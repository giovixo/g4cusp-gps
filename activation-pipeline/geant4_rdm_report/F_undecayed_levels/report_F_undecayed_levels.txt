Radioactive decay: 155 long-lived levels are never decayed correctly, and no
warning is given
==============================================================================

- Components: processes/hadronic/models/radioactive_decay
  (G4VRadioactiveDecay::LoadDecayTable, DecayIt, data RadioactiveDecay6.1.2
  and 6.1.3d) and particles (G4IonTable::GetIon, G4IonTable::FindIon,
  G4NuclideTable::GetIsotope)
- Geant4 version: 11.4.1 (datasets G4ENSDFSTATE3.0, RadioactiveDecay6.1.2 and
  6.1.3d, PhotonEvaporation6.1.2)
- Platform: macOS 15.8, Apple silicon (arm64)
- Severity: decay radiation is lost, or comes from another level with another
  lifetime; no warning

All numbers are for long-lived levels (1 s <= T1/2 < 10^15 s) and for
RadioactiveDecay6.1.3 (04/11/2026, bug 2794). 
Where the 6.1.2 numbers differ, they are given in parentheses. 
Geant4 overall behavior is the same with both datasets.

The levels are taken from G4ENSDFSTATE3.0. 
According to their History files, G4ENSDFSTATE3.0 and RadioactiveDecay6.x were both generated 
from the March 2024 ENSDF data. The two files should therefore describe the same set of levels: 
6493 of the 6552 P records of 6.1.3 match an ENSDFSTATE.dat level.

Summary
-------

In Geant4, a level is decayed correctly only if two lookups succeed:

1. G4IonTable::GetIon(Z, A, E, flb) creates the requested level.

2. G4VRadioactiveDecay::LoadDecayTable finds the decay data of that
   level in RadioactiveDecay. 
   The energy and the floating-level base (+X, +Y, ...) must both match.

These lookups fail, without a warning, for two sets of levels:

- 	No decay data (section 1): 139 levels (147 in 6.1.2). 
	In these, no P record in the library matches them.
	
- 	Levels never created (section 2): 99 levels. 
	These are E = 0 levels. Some nuclides have more than one level at E = 0: 
	a ground state plus an isomer whose excitation energy is unknown (0+X), 
	or two states whose order is unknown (0+X, 0+Y). 
	With E = 0, G4IonTable ignores the floating-level base and always returns the 
	first E = 0 level listed in ENSDFSTATE.dat. 
	A request for any other E = 0 level of the nuclide returns that first level, 
	with its lifetime and decay data, which usually differ from those of 
	the originally requested level.
	
	A level energy is measured through the transitions that link the level to the
	ground state. When no transition links a group of levels to the rest of the
	scheme, ENSDF gives their energies relative to an unknown base: E+X, and Y, Z,
	U, V, W... for further independent groups (ENSDF manual, field E).
	G4ENSDFSTATE3.0 and RadioactiveDecay store only the number as the energy and
	the letter in a separate column. 
	E.g., Ta178 0+X and Ta178 0+Y both have E = 0, and only the letter tells them apart. 


83 levels (90 in 6.1.2) are in both sets, so 139 + 99 - 83 = 155 levels (156 in 6.1.2) 
in total are affected. 

What happens then with Geant4 can be classified in these categories:

A. 	Geant4 creates the level but there is no decay data.                      
	Result: killed at its decay time, no radiation       
	Levels: 55 (56)  
	Examples: Ta178[0X] (2.36 h), Cm252 (2.0 d), Bi198 (10.3 min)
	
	One special sub-case is Pm152[150X] (13.8 min) which is de-excited by IT instead of its beta- decay.
	
B.  Geant4 creates another level instead, one with decay data.
	Result: wrong lifetime and radiation                 
	Levels: 87 (86)  
	Examples: Sb120[0X] (5.76 d) decays as Sb120 (15.9 min); Np240[0X] (7.22 min) as Np240 (1.03 h)

C.	Geant4 creates another level instead, one without decay data  
	Result: no radiation                                 
	Levels: 12 (13)  
	Examples: Ta178[0Y] (9.31 min) becomes Ta178[0X]; Bk248[0X] (23.7 h) becomes Bk248[0Z] (9 y)

In total, 67 levels (69) (A, except Pm152[150X], and C) emit no radiation at all, 
and 88 (87) (Pm152[150X] + B) emit the radiation of the wrong level or decay mode.

Case C includes the worst example, the W178 chain. 
W178 (T1/2 21.6 d) decays 100 % by EC to Ta178[0Y] (1+, 9.31 min). 
Ta178[0Y] has decay data, but Geant4 cannot create it; it creates Ta178[0X] (7-, 2.36 h) instead, 
which has no decay data. 
All Ta178 radiation of the W178 chain is therefore lost: the Hf X-rays, 
the 93 keV gamma and the beta+ branch.

There are three paths in Geant4 which request levels that cannot be created (cases B and C):
- radioactive decay: a daughter is created with the floating-level base given
  in the decay record (e.g. G4ECDecay.cc:63). Eight decays of parents with
  T1/2 >= 1 s feed such a level (section 2);
- hadronic de-excitation: G4PhotonEvaporation keeps the floating index of the
  level where the cascade ends (G4PhotonEvaporation.cc:585-586), and
  G4ExcitationHandler passes it to GetIon (G4ExcitationHandler.cc:554-561). A
  spallation or capture residue that ends on such an isomer is created as the
  first E = 0 level;
- direct user requests, e.g. /gun/ion 73 178 0 0 Y.

The latest 6.1.3 (bug 2794) added decay data for six of these isomers
(Tb154[0X], Tb154[0Y], Ir186[0X], Sb128[0X], Np240[0X], Tc102[0X]). 
However, Geant4 cannot use them, because it never actually creates these levels (section 2).

Attached:
- 	check_undecayed_levels.py: a standard-library Python script. 
	It lists 
	(1) the levels without decay data, 
	(2) the E = 0 levels that cannot be created,
	(3) the decay records that feed them 
	It can also write an rdecay01 macro for the levels of list 1 and classify the rdecay01 output;
- check_undecayed_levels_6.1.3.out: its output on RadioactiveDecay6.1.3,
  including the classification of the rdecay01 run;
- check_undecayed_levels.out: the same on 6.1.2;
- rdecay01_undecayed_levels.mac: a short macro for the standard example
  extended/radioactivedecay/rdecay01;
- rdecay01_all_levels.mac: the macro for the 139 levels of list 1 (6.1.3),
  written by the script.



Section 1. Levels that can be created but without decay data: 139 (147 in 6.1.2)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For an E = 0 level with no matching P record, LoadDecayTable returns an empty
decay table, and DecayIt kills the nucleus at its decay time without
secondaries (the "has no decays" branch). 
For an excited level with no P record, LoadDecayTable instead adds an IT channel with 
branching 1 (G4VRadioactiveDecay.cc:877-880). 
There is no warning in either case, although the level has a finite ENSDFSTATE lifetime.

Each of the 139 levels can be created with /gun/ion Z A 0 E flb in rdecay01:
- 	55 (56) are created as requested and emit nothing in any decay (case A above).
- 	1 is created and de-excited by IT (again, case A above): Pm152[150X] (T1/2 13.8 min). 
	It is an excited level with no P record, so it is de-excited through PhotonEvaporation 
	levels (gammas, Pm152 at 44.45, 25.02 and 16.03 keV) to the Pm152 ground state, 
	which then decays with its own 4.1 min half-life.
  	(By the way: in NUBASE2020 this level decays beta- 100%, IT not established).
- 	83 (90) are never created: they are E = 0 levels in Section 2, 
	and Geant4 creates another level instead (cases B and C).

The case A levels with the longest half-lives are:

    Ta178[0X]  2.36 h

Compared with 6.1.2, the latest 6.1.3 includes decay data for eight more levels: 
Hg187, which is now decayed correctly, and seven shadowed E = 0 levels reported in section 2 
(the six isomers listed in the summary and Ho154[0X]), 
which are still never created. 
The other levels are unchanged. 
Ta178[0X] data existed in RadioactiveDecay5.6 (P 0 +X 8496, EC to the 1147.4 and 1479.0 keV 
levels of Hf178) and is missing from 6.1.2 and 6.1.3d.

Cause
-----

- 	Section 1 (no decay data). 
	For a level with a finite ENSDFSTATE lifetime and
	no matching P record, LoadDecayTable returns an empty decay table if E = 0,
	and DecayIt then kills the nucleus without secondaries (case A). 
	If E > 0, it adds an IT channel (G4VRadioactiveDecay.cc:877-880), 
	whatever the real decay mode of the level (Pm152[150X] case). Neither issues a warning.

-	Section 2 (never created). 
	At E = 0 the floating-level base is dropped in three places:
	
		1. G4NuclideTable::GetIsotope (G4NuclideTable.cc:118) 
		accepts a level if flb == level->GetFloatLevelBase() || E == 0.0. 
		So at E = 0 it returns the first level of the multimap, i.e. the first in ENSDFSTATE.dat.
		
		2. G4IonTable::GetIon (G4IonTable.cc:483-490)
		when no ion exists yet, it takes the floating base of that level (flb1) and creates 
		the ion with it (CreateIon(Z, A, E, flb1), line 500).
		
		3. G4IonTable::FindIon (G4IonTable.cc:644-648) 
		has a "rerun search on ground level without check on floating": 
		at E = 0 it returns the first existing ion with E = 0, whatever its floating base.

In addition, all E = 0 levels of a nuclide get the same PDG encoding.
G4NuclideTable raises the isomer level only for E > 0 (if (ionE > 0) ++iLevel,
G4NuclideTable.cc:211), and CreateIon sets lvl = 0 when Eex == 0
(G4IonTable.cc:277). 
The encoding computed at line 287 is therefore the same for all of them.

LoadDecayTable, by contrast, requires the floating base of the P record to
match that of the ion (G4VRadioactiveDecay.cc:611-618). 
So the decay data written for a level that is never created are never used, and the level
created in its place may have no data at all.



Effect in an application
------------------------

In our activation study for a high-energy astrophysics CubeSat experiment
(5-700 MeV protons on a satellite mass model, Geant4 11.4.1), W178 is significantly
produced in the tungsten collimators, in secular equilibrium with its daughter Ta178 (1+).
These contribute 0.345 Bq out of 35.7 Bq of payload activity after one year in orbit. 
All of its radiation is lost. 
The missing radiation is a small but sizable fraction of the overall background.



Possible fixes
--------------

- 	In G4NuclideTable::GetIsotope and G4IonTable::FindIon, ignore the floating
  	base at E = 0 only when the request has none (flb == no_Float). A request
  	with a letter should get the level with that letter.
	
- 	Give each E = 0 floating level its own isomer level (and PDG encoding), 
	as for E > 0 levels.
	
- 	Add decay data for the levels in section 1. 
	For Ta178[0X], the RadioactiveDecay5.6 block could be restored.
	
- 	Issue a warning (or a JustWarning G4Exception, once per nuclide):
  		- 	in LoadDecayTable or DecayIt, when an unstable nucleus with a finite
    		ENSDFSTATE lifetime has no decay channel;
  		- 	in GetIon, when the level returned has a floating base other than 
			the one requested.
