Activation-induced background of the CUSP polarimeter

Background from the decay of nuclides produced by trapped protons in the CUSP mass model, for the SPENVIS orbit, computed with the three-step method of Campana et al. (2026). The simulation is the same for every orbit; only the proton flux changes. Rates are averaged over the out-of-belt part of the orbit unless stated otherwise.

Background during the first day

Instantaneous activation-induced rate at 60 s resolution from the start of the mission (no prior activation). Each passage through the South Atlantic Anomaly (shaded) builds up short-lived activity, which decays within tens of minutes; the baseline between passages rises slowly as longer-lived nuclides accumulate. The prompt background during the passages is not part of this calculation.

Long-term out-of-belt background, first 5 years

Out-of-belt mean rate over a trailing 30-day window, from the 60 s history of the first 5 years. The rise comes from nuclides with half-lives of months to years (Na22, Gd153, Gd151 and others), still far from saturation after 5 years for the longest-lived ones.

Background spectrum after one year

Out-of-belt mean spectrum after one year in orbit, shown in 4 keV bins. The 511 keV annihilation line dominates all three classes; GAGG singles also show the activation lines of the crystal itself below 300 keV, Na22 at 1274.5 keV and Na24 at 1368.6 keV. The narrow feature at 1732 keV in the plastic is the double-escape peak of the 2754 keV Na24 gamma.

Method and limits

  • Detector response: true energy deposits; no resolution, light yield or quenching. Electronics dead time and pile-up are not modelled.
  • Coverage: the (volume, nuclide) pairs below the step-3 activity threshold (about 0.9% of the out-of-belt activity) were not simulated in step 4; rates are low by about that fraction.
  • Geant4 data: decays follow Geant4's own decay data, including the known RadioactiveDecay6.1.2 errors (Geant4 bug 2780 and related reports). Neutron activation changes the yields by less than 3 × 10-5.
  • Statistics: the statistical error of the total rates is below 10-3 (relative); single weak pairs are noisier. All orbits use the same step-4 spectra, so the ratios between orbits carry almost no statistical error.
